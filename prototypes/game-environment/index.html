Degrees of Freedom: The Game

Four ways to build a structure

Each of these is a working prototype of the game environment. They share the same three player tools (connections, member geometry, loads) and produce the same solver input. What differs is how building feels.

Prototypes for comparison. They need an internet connection the first time, to load three.js. Use a current Chrome or Edge.

Grow

Pull members out of joints

Click a joint and the points it can reach bloom around it. Click one and a member pops in. Free 3D camera.

Asks
Can free joint-to-joint building in 3D be quick and unambiguous?
Gives
The most freedom: any of 26 directions, lengths of 1 to 4 steps.
Costs
Depth is judged by eye, and there are many targets on screen at once.
Open Grow

Levels

Draw one level at a time

An isometric board with one glowing working level. Step through levels like floors and draw on the active one.

Asks
Does a working level make 3D building precise and calm enough for turns?
Gives
Exact picking, any plan angle, a view that reads like a board.
Costs
More steps for anything vertical. No free camera.
Open Levels

Blocks

Stack bays, the frame appears

Click to stack bays. Columns and beams appear along their edges. Then brace panels, pin joints and drop loads.

Asks
Is "massing first, engineering second" a better loop for this game?
Gives
A structure in seconds. The closest to the one-click building that inspired the look.
Costs
Member length is fixed by the 2 m bay.
Open Blocks

Kit

Take turns playing pieces from a tray

A board game. Each turn a player takes sticks, joints and weights from a tray and plays them onto a shared structure.

Asks
What does turn-based play look like on screen? Is length better chosen as a piece?
Gives
The only option that shows turns, players and a hand of pieces.
Costs
Slower building. Its turn rules are placeholders, not decisions.
Open Kit

What all four share

  • One model, in shared/model-core.js. Every option edits it and shows its draft solver input in a drawer ("Solver input").
  • No solver is connected. The "feedback preview" in each option uses placeholder numbers and says so on screen.
  • Pieces with no path to a support are always shown differently. That is a plain connectivity check, not a stability result.
